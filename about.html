About Hood Research

Hood Research is a student-led research club focused on exploring mathematics and physics through collaboration, rigorous problem solving, and independent research.

General

1.1. Name The name of this organization is the Hood Research Institute. Henceforth, it will be simply called “The Institute.” 1.2. Membership Any University of Toronto student passionate about mathematics and mathematical-physics research is able to join the Institute. 1.3. Rights All members of the institute have the right to: Participate in any general event held by the Institute Vote in any general election or referendum held by the Institute Volunteer to help run events Share potential research ideas to executive officers Participate in research run by the Institute Only executive officers can vote in executive meetings

Selection of Executive Officers

2.1. Modes of selection All executive officers except for the Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute and Principal Commissioner of Mathematical Affairs shall be selected through election in every year after the 2026-2027 school year. During the 2026-2027 school year, the executive officers will be declared by the institute at the start of the year. All executive officers up for election shall be elected at elections held in late September. Supplementary elections may be called at the discretion of the Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute in the case of vacant executive officer seats. The Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute and Principal Commissioner of Mathematical Affairs shall be selected by the executive officers of the previous year in late spring. 2.2. Electorate All members of the Hood Research Department are eligible to vote in elections. 2.3. Eligibility Any member of the Institute is eligible to run for an officer position given the following: Any member seeking an officer position must have been recognized for distinguished service by the Institute or granted an exemption from the distinguished service requirement by the Head of Talent and Resource Acquisition Any member seeking an officer position must nominate themselves or be nominated by another member of The Institute. Nomination can either occur through a Google Form or communication with the Head of Talent and Resource application via Discord. 2.4. Nomination period Before the general election in September, there must be a nomination period lasting no less than one week. A candidate may either nominate themselves or be nominated by another member of the Institute. A nomination requires the candidate's full name, UTORID or student number, student email, and a statement of the candidate's research interests. 2.5. Campaigning Candidates may begin campaigning as soon as the nomination period begins. Candidates are permitted to campaign on behalf of another candidate in so far as no officer uses their position to campaign for any candidate. 2.6. Election Procedure 2.6.1 September Election The September election is to be held annually in late September to elect members of the Institute to officer positions, which are up for election Elections for each open seat will be held simultaneously. 2.6.2. Snap Elections A snap election may be called by the Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute and Head of Talent and Resource Acquisition in case of a vacancy that arises during the academic year. 2.6.3. General Procedure All elections must be conducted in person by secret ballot. Each member may submit up to one general ballot which contains the member’s chosen vote for each position up for election. Each ballot will count equally. To vote, each member must present their student I.D. before submitting their ballot. A member may have someone vote for them by proxy. The proxy must present written evidence that the voting member has consented for the proxy to vote on their behalf. The default voting system is ranked choice voting with the ability to rank up to 3 candidates. If a tie occurs at the end of ranked choice voting, then a math duel will occur between the candidates. In case of a math duel, each side can choose 2 mathematics textbooks from which potential problems will be selected. Then, from each mathematics textbook in this pool, the VP of Contests and Duels will use a random number generator to select precisely 2 questions from each textbook. Whichever candidate can finish all the problems first, correctness graded by the VP of Contests and Duels, in an environment supervised by the VP of Contests and Duels will win the seat. In the scenario that the VP of Contests and Duals has a conflict of interest, to be decided by the Principal Commissioner of Mathematical Affairs and the Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute jointly, they may be replaced by the Supreme Executive Director of Mathematical Affairs. In the extreme scenario that neither the VP of Contests and Duals, Principal Commissioner of Mathematical Affairs, nor Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute are without a conflict of interest, a qualified member of the Institute will be chosen at random to supervise and mark the math duel. In the scenario that a candidate believes an executive involved in the math duel has an undeclared conflict of interest, they may file a petition to be voted on by all members of the Council of Eight excluding those who have alleged conflicts of interest. 2.7. Vacant Seats If a seat becomes vacant during the academic year outside of the election period(s), the Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute may hold a snap election to fill the seat. The snap election will be organized by the Head of Talent and Resource Acquisition. The Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute , with the agreement of the Head of Talent and Resource Acquisition, may form an ad hoc committee of the Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute, Head of Talent and Resource Acquisition, and Minister of Internal Affairs to fill the vacant seat. If the office of the Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute is vacant, the Principal Commissioner of Mathematical Affairs shall take the seat of Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute and promote an ordinary elected executive officer to the position of Principal Commissioner of Mathematical Affairs.

Executive Officers

3.1. Term The term length of Executive Officers is based on an internal voting system. The Hood Research Institute considers one term to be one academic year. 3.2. Eligibility All officers must satisfy the membership criteria of the Hood Research Department for the length of their term in office. 3.3. Position and Duties The executive officers of this student union shall consist of the following: Collectively, the Council of Eight form The Eight Distinguished Vice-Regal Commissioners for the Advancement of Mathematical, Academic, Social, and Institutional Affairs of the Hood Research Institute In Order of Rankings: Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute Principal Commissioner of Mathematical Affairs Supreme Executive Director of Mathematical Ideological Development High Commissioner for Academic Affairs and Research Minister of Internal Order and External Relations Minister for Social Affairs, Cultural Programming, and Institute Festivities Director of State Assets, Material Resources, and Institutional Property Student Compliance and MAT158/159 Integration Each member of The Eight Distinguished Vice-Regal Commissioners for the Advancement of Mathematical, Academic, Social, and Institutional Affairs of the Hood Research Institute has subroles to ensure full working efficiency. These roles include, but are not limited to: Head of Talent and Resource Acquisition Chief of Research Beyond Current Horizons Chief Science Officer VP of Contests and Duels 3.31. Supreme Director-General of the Hood Research Institute It shall be the duty of the Supreme Director-General to: Exercise ultimate executive authority over the Hood Research Institute and preserve the continued advancement, stability, and prosperity of The Institute Foster a welcoming community for members of the Institute from all mathematical backgrounds Guarantee the rights of all members of the Institute Coordinate Institute programming and research with the Council of Eight Exercise discretion in matters concerning the implementation of this Constitution where no explicit provision exists Maintain familiarity with all applicable University of Toronto policies, student union regulations, Institute protocols, and other governing documents. Maintain oversight of the Institute's physical spaces, resources, finances, membership, and research activities. In the event of a vacant executive seat, exercise the powers granted under Section 2.7. Oversee the orderly conduct of elections, snap elections, and succession procedures as prescribed by this Constitution. Ensure the preservation and transfer of institutional knowledge to future Executive Councils. 3.32. Principal Commissioner of Mathematical Affairs It shall be the duty of the Principal Commissioner of Mathematical Affairs to: Dictate and coordinate the research directions of the Institute Coordinate with the Supreme Director of Mathematical Ideological Development to create and organize research-related events for members of the Institute, including lectures, colloquia, and workshops. Stay knowledgeable about the research activities of the Institute Assist in the administration of any formal mathematical duel conducted under Section 2.6.3 where requested. Develop initiatives intended to improve the mathematical abilities and confidence of members of the Institute. Encourage members to submit potential research ideas to the Executive Directorate. Review mathematical research proposals submitted by members and refer promising proposals to the appropriate research authorities. Maintain awareness of developments, opportunities, conferences, competitions, and research programs relevant to undergraduate mathematics. Assist in identifying members deserving of distinguished service recognition. Undertake such additional mathematical responsibilities as may be assigned by the Supreme Director-General. 3.33. Supreme Executive Director of Mathematical Ideological Development It shall be the duty of the Supreme Executive Director of Mathematical Ideological Development to: Direct the Institute's official communications, publicity, and information dissemination apparatus. Manage day-to-day communication between the Executive Directorate, the Hood Research Department, the constituency, and external organizations. Maintain the Institute's online presence across University of Toronto platforms, student portals, Discord servers, social media, and other approved communication channels. Ensure that official information regarding Institute events, elections, research opportunities, and other activities is communicated to the constituency in a timely manner. Establish and maintain a coherent public identity for the Hood Research Institute. Coordinate the Institute's social media presence with the Minister for Social Affairs, Cultural Programming, and Institute Festivities. Ensure continuous photographic and video documentation of Institute events and significant institutional developments. Ensure that communications accurately reflect decisions made by the Executive Directorate. Maintain an archive of significant Institute communications for the benefit of future Executive Councils. Chair a Communications and Propaganda Committee where deemed necessary by the Executive Directorate. Assist the Head of Talent and Resource Acquisition in publicizing applications, nominations, elections, and recruitment opportunities. 3.34. High Commissioner for Academic Affairs and Research It shall be the duty of the High Commissioner for Academic Affairs and Research to: Direct The Institute's academic and research-oriented programming. Organize academic seminars, lectures, workshops, study sessions, and research information sessions. Organize tutorials and educational programming in subjects and tools relevant to the constituency, including LaTeX, Python, mathematical software, and research methodology. Maintain awareness of relevant academic opportunities and ensure that information concerning such opportunities is shared with the constituency. Promote participation in research programs, conferences, competitions, mentorship programs, and other academic opportunities. Encourage members to submit research ideas to The Institute. Develop procedures for reviewing and forwarding promising research ideas to the appropriate Executive Officers or research personnel. Ensure that academic programming remains accessible to students with varying levels of mathematical experience. 3.35. Minister of Internal Order and External Relations It shall be the duty of the Minister of Internal Order and External Relations to: Maintain the overall cleanliness, order, and general upkeep of Institute spaces. Ensure that issues affecting Institute facilities are reported and addressed in a timely manner. Pursue potential collaborations with other student unions, academic societies, departments, and external organizations. Serve as the principal liaison between the Institute and organizations with which the Institute maintains formal or informal relations. Assist in outreach to students outside the formal mathematics program who participate in mathematics courses. Support the integration of new members into the Institute and its traditions. Assist in the resolution of internal disputes before such disputes escalate to the Supreme Director-General or other formal mechanisms. 3.36. Minister for Social Affairs, Cultural Programming, and Institute Festivities It shall be the duty of the Minister for Social Affairs, Cultural Programming, and Institute Festivities to: Organize general social events and community gatherings. Organize larger-scale Institute celebrations. Coordinate social programming with the Minister of Internal Order and External Relations. Coordinate the Institute's social media presence with the Supreme Executive Director of Mathematical Ideological Development. Publicize social events in a timely manner. Ensure that Institute events are welcoming and accessible to members of the constituency. Work to resolve conflicts that may prevent members from participating in Institute activities. Develop programming intended to strengthen community, camaraderie, and morale within the Institute. Coordinate volunteers and other members assisting with social programming. Maintain an annual calendar of Institute festivities and traditions. Preserve notable Institute traditions for the benefit of future Executive Councils. Encourage participation in social events by newly admitted members and students entering the Institute for the first time. 3.37. Director of State Assets, Material Resources, and Institutional Property It shall be the duty of the Director of State Assets, Material Resources, and Institutional Property to: Maintain a continuous and accurate inventory of all physical property belonging to or entrusted to the Hood Research Institute. Ensure a consistent supply of goods required for the operation of the Institute. Monitor the distribution and sale of consumable goods and report relevant revenues to the financial authorities of the Institute. Ensure that all Institute equipment remains functional and properly maintained. Organize fundraising and merchandise initiatives in consultation with the Executive Directorate. Maintain a comprehensive inventory of Institute property. Ensure that Institute spaces are secured appropriately when closed or unattended. Report lost, damaged, stolen, or otherwise compromised Institute property to the Supreme Director-General. Advise the Executive Directorate regarding acquisition, replacement, disposal, and maintenance of Institute assets. Maintain appropriate records for all Institute property so that assets may be accounted for during the transfer of power between Executive Councils. Cooperate with the Treasurer or other designated financial authority in matters concerning the Institute's financial position and expenditure. 3.38. Student Compliance and MAT158/159 Integration It shall be the duty of the Commissioner for Student Compliance and MAT158/159 Integration to: Serve as the principal representative for first-year students and students enrolled in MAT158/159. Maintain regular communication with first-year members of the constituency. Facilitate the integration of new students into the Hood Research Institute and the broader mathematical community. Organize events and initiatives specifically intended for first-year students and students enrolled in MAT158/159. Assist first-year students in becoming familiar with academic, social, research, and institutional resources. Work with the Minister for Social Affairs, Cultural Programming, and Institute Festivities to develop social programming that encourages first-year participation. Coordinate with the Minister of Internal Order and External Relations regarding outreach to students outside the formal mathematics program who are enrolled in MAT158/159. Encourage first-year students to participate in Institute events, competitions, research opportunities, and other activities. Maintain an appropriate channel through which first-year students may submit concerns, suggestions, and requests.

Meetings

4.1. General Meetings General meetings of the Institute shall be open to all members of the Institute. General meetings shall be called and organized by the Supreme Director-General and Minister of Internal Order and External Relations. At a general meeting, any member of the Institute may raise concerns to the Council of Eight, present petitions, ask questions of executive officers, and present feedback to the executive officers. 4.2. Research Gatherings The Institute shall run, at least once a month and, if possible, once every two weeks, a general research gathering. A research gathering may consist of a lecture, workshop, colloquium, or any other gathering of members of the Institute to discuss the Institute’s research. Research gatherings must be an accepting and comfortable environment for members of the Institute to socialize both in a formal and an informal setting. As such, research gatherings may generally have an initial event at an Institute location followed by an after-event informal discussion in another location. Other locations may include restaurants, parks, etc. The goal of research gatherings is to further the Institute’s research goals by encouraging collaboration across different groups and projects. 4.3 Executive Meetings Executive meetings shall be held once a month either in person or via an online platform e.g. Discord or Instagram 4.3.1 Decisions and Votes Decisions will be made by a majority vote of the members of the Council of Eight unless the Supreme Director-General specifies otherwise. Comments by executive officers outside of the Council of Eight are always welcome and will be considered by the Council of Eight in their vote. A decision may be vetoed if and only if both the Supreme Director General and Principle Commissioner of Mathematical Affairs believe that it will cause unnecessary hardship upon the members of the Institute, go against the Institute’s constitution, or cause undue harm to the Institute’s mission. If there is a tie vote of the Council of Eight or greater body of executive officers, the Supreme Director-General and the Principle Commissioner of Mathematical Affairs must agree on a decision. If they can not agree, the decision will go to a general vote of the Institute’s members at a general meeting held as soon as possible.

Removal from Office

5.1. Impeachment If an executive officer, including any member of the Council of Eight, is deemed to have acted in such a way as to break the Institute’s constitution or the rules of any body of which the Institute is a member or acted in such a way as to significantly harm the Institute, they may be impeached and removed from office. 5.2. Procedure An anonymous motion for impeachment may be put forward to the Supreme Director General by any executive officer of the Institute. As a result of an impeachment motion, a trial will be held. The defense will be the officer who is to be impeached, and the prosecution shall be the accuser or, if the accuser does not wish to prosecute, any member of the institute willing to prosecute may step in as prosecutor. The trial shall be mediated by VP of Contests and Duals as the judge. In the scenario that the VP of Contests and Duals has a conflict of interest, they may be replaced by any member of the Council of Eight. Each party in the trial may allow additional members of the Institute to serve as support on their side of the bench. However, these support parties may not directly question witnesses, present evidence, or petition the judge. They will only be allowed to present a short, brief of their interest in the trial at the beginning of the trial. During the trial, each party will open arguments with an opening argument, the length of which shall be determined by the judge. After the opening arguments, the prosecution will present its witnesses. Any member of the institute called upon as a witness must show up for the trial and answer all questions earnestly and honestly. The prosecution will be permitted to ask its witnesses questions before cross- examination by the defense. Each party may object to the opposing party’s questions if the objections fall under: relevance, hearsay, speculation, argumentative, beyond the scope. The judge must make a decision on each objection before questioning can continue. After the prosecution has gone through all of its witnesses, the defense is then able to present its witnesses. The defense will start with questioning before cross-examination by the prosecution. All other rules from the prosecution's questioning apply. After all witnesses are questioned, both prosecution and defense will be given the chance to present a closing argument. After the closing arguments, the Council of Eight members not involved as either prosecution, defense, or judge will deliberate. The Council shall decide on a majority basis for ordinary executives and super majority basis for Supreme Director General and Principle Commissioner of Mathematical Affairs. If any executive except for the Principle Commissioner for Mathematical Affairs is impeached, their post shall be filled by the usual rules outlined in this constitution. If the Principle Commissioner for Mathematical Affairs is impeached, the remaining members of the Council of Eight shall convene to promote an ordinary non-executive member of the Institute to the position via majority vote.
